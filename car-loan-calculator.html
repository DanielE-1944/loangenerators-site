How car loan payments are calculated

A car loan is a fixed-term (amortizing) loan: you borrow the car's price minus your down payment, and repay it in equal monthly installments that include both principal and interest. Early in the loan, more of each payment goes toward interest; over time, more goes toward paying down the principal. The three numbers that matter most are the loan amount, the annual interest rate, and the loan term ― a longer term lowers your monthly payment but increases the total interest you pay over the life of the loan.

Tips for getting a better car loan

A larger down payment reduces both your monthly payment and total interest, since you're borrowing less to begin with. Shorter loan terms (36-48 months instead of 60-72) usually carry lower interest rates and cost less overall, even though the monthly payment is higher. It's also worth comparing pre-approved offers from a bank or credit union against dealership financing before signing anything ― rates can vary significantly between lenders for the same borrower.

Frequently asked questions

How do you calculate a car loan payment?

It's based on the loan amount (price minus down payment), the annual interest rate, and the term in months, split into equal payments that cover interest and principal.

What is a good interest rate for a car loan?

It depends on your credit score, the loan term, and whether the car is new or used. Comparing several lenders is the best way to find a competitive rate.

Does a bigger down payment lower my monthly payment?

Yes ― a larger down payment shrinks the loan principal, which lowers both your monthly payment and the total interest paid.

Can the down payment be paid at the end of the loan instead?

Yes ― this is a balloon payment structure. The full price is financed, monthly payments are lower, and the deferred down payment is due as one lump sum at the end of the term. Use the toggle above the calculator to try this option.
